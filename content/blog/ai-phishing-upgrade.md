AI-powered impersonation attacks are on the rise — and they're getting easier. As executives and senior leaders increasingly rely on generative AI to draft Slack messages and emails, they're quietly dismantling one of the most reliable defenses organizations have against social engineering: the human communication fingerprint.

People develop distinct writing quirks over years. Short, clipped messages. Never capitalizing the first word. Heavy use of abbreviations. These patterns form an informal but surprisingly effective authentication layer that threat actors have historically struggled to replicate.

That's changing fast.
